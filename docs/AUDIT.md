# Hostile SDET audit

This audit began with a clean `codex/tradeflow-lab` working tree at `5897203757ee751c7febbe36f8e42ef2788a0668`. It examines a synthetic interview lab, not a production trading platform. The audit fixes are local; no push, merge, `main` creation, default-branch change, or visibility change is authorized in this step. Human acceptance of generated behavior and tests remains separate from these checks.

## Questions that still expose weak understanding

1. Why did an API-only negative test execute authentication through `storageState`, even though localStorage never supplied its bearer header?
2. How do fixture dependency resolution, test scope, worker scope, and teardown order differ from project selection and workers?
3. Why does a request-generation guard need session identity and error-path ownership too?
4. How can server book isolation still leave another session's rows rendered in the browser?
5. What does POST 201 followed by GET 500 mean for mutation outcome, feedback, and duplicate risk?
6. Which removed predicates/constraints did the original tests fail to detect, and why did green counts conceal that weakness?
7. Why is AJV against live OpenAPI independent of Zod, and where do their acceptance sets still diverge?
8. How do retry results, `failOnFlakyTests`, initial-attempt evidence, and first-retry traces interact?
9. What was actually established by the hosted artifact upload, and what remains unproved on a failed job?
10. Why can this closed-loop local performance smoke not justify a throughput, latency SLO, or production-capacity claim?

Study hardest: Playwright's fixture dependency/lifetime graph; async session/UI ownership; negative/state/mutation oracles; OpenAPI/runtime validation and HTTP boundaries; hosted CI diagnostics and defensible performance evidence. Understand these from the executed source and failures, rather than memorizing interview answers.
